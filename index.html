Every major federal contract requires a designated government watchdog — the Contracting Officer's Representative. FAC-COR Level III is the ceiling: the credential authorizing oversight of the most complex, highest-dollar, and most mission-critical contracts in the federal system. Fewer than a fraction of federal employees hold it.

For private-sector context, think of it as a certified operations authority with legal oversight responsibility for vendor performance, compliance, and accountability — disciplines that transfer directly into any organization managing contracts, vendors, or regulated programs. Nationwide QC Framework — HUD Field Service Management

HUD's Field Service Management program relies on private contractors to secure, maintain, and prepare government-owned residential properties for disposition. As Technical Monitor (GTM) overseeing 10+ FSM contracts representing $50M in managed real estate, William found that monthly QC reports were inconsistent — metrics varied by contractor, accountability was uneven, and compliance gaps were going undetected until they became liabilities.

He revised the Quality Control Plan from the ground up — establishing exactly what contractors had to track, how to document it, and when. The framework set enforceable performance thresholds across five areas: HPIR inspection uploads (2-day requirement), Health & Safety emergency response (24-hour abatement), Initial Clean-Out timelines (5-day), bi-weekly Routine Inspections, and Environmental Compliance (lead-based paint and wood-destroying organisms). Result: a 22% improvement in contractor compliance — and a monitoring system that gave HUD leadership the right data to act before problems escalated.

QC Monthly Report — Framework Excerpt · HUD Field Service Management PURPOSE & SCOPE This Quality Control Plan establishes standardized monthly reporting requirements for all prime FSM contractors. Each submission covers a representative sample (~3%) of properties active in the prior two months, with full case file review in HUD's P260 system (Steps 1–8).

PERFORMANCE STANDARDS MONITORED
· HPIR Compliance — Parts I, II & III uploaded to P260 within 2 business days
· Health & Safety — Emergency work orders abated within 24 hours of identification
· Initial Clean-Out — Services completed within 5 business days of assignment
· Routine Inspections — Conducted on bi-weekly cycle for all assigned properties
· Environmental Compliance — LBP & WDO work orders issued within 1 business day
